# Adaptation examples

| Project | Video opening | Evidence | Caption / cover |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product demo | A task the viewer struggles with | Real task recording and outcome | Useful result and real product state |
| Tutorial | One specific question | Reproducible worked example | Clear takeaway and readable result |
| Small business | A customer situation | Original footage with permission | Local voice and concrete details |
| Educational story | A supportable observation | Checked sources and visible explanation | Explain the insight with representative imagery |
| Movie language learning | A complete expression in context | Permitted source and verified quote | Meaning/context; optional bilingual movie tags |

## Optional movie-learning profile

A Chinese-speaking English-learning brand might choose conversational Chinese captions, a verified short expression, and a cinematic cover with central ivory text and an orange keyword. It might use 3:4 covers with wide margins and a center-square crop check while exporting 9:16 videos. These choices belong in its profile.

For content actually featuring *Spirited Away*, that brand could require separate `#千与千寻` and `#SpiritedAway` tags. Verify every featured title pair; add no invented film association to general product posts. Check source permissions instead of assuming film clips are licensed.

Other brands can use original recordings, interviews, diagrams, or generated scenes in their own languages, formats, voice, and cadence.
