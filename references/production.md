# From idea to final video

Reuse known choices; ask only for missing information that materially affects the work. Establish audience, one takeaway, intended action, available sources, format, and requested deliverable (ideas, script, preview, or finished video).

Choose the story action before decorating it: a failed task becomes successful, a question gets a real demonstration, or a short story has a visible outcome. When a different style is requested, change narrative structure, viewer participation, visual medium, shot organization, or sound rhythm meaningfully.

Write a timeline naming picture/action, on-screen words, speech/sound, and transitions. Include opening promise, evidence, payoff, and a proportionate next step. Duration follows actual reading and speech needs; never truncate a sentence to fit a template.

## Production choices

- Use real product captures/verified data. Label mockups and reenactments; invented controls must not appear to be real functionality.
- Record sources and permission status. Public access is not proof of commercial reuse permission. Use original, licensed, public-domain, or otherwise permitted material for the intended use.
- Preserve editable projects and manual changes. Version substantial revisions.
- Add narration when appropriate to the brief. Verify a requested model/voice's availability and cost authorization instead of substituting silently.
- Preserve important dialogue, effects, and pauses. If native platform music is planned, avoid a competing baked-in track. Supply existing music facts and dialogue time ranges.
- Follow the available editor's instructions. No particular editor or generation service is required by this skill.

## Final checks

Inspect frames across the complete timeline, openings/endings, transitions, subtitles, text edges, real product actions, and rapid motion. Listen for complete speech, timing, abrupt cuts, music masking speech, and clipping. Probes cannot verify these qualities.

The helper checks a real video stream, nonzero duration, and full FFmpeg decode. A reviewer separately records observations. Honor the selected final-approval policy; an authorized direct-completion/final-upload policy can satisfy it. Do not make final approval a universal extra step.

Deliver a playable video, editable source when available, factual brief, QA observations, and job ID. Drafts and alternatives stay out of publishing.
